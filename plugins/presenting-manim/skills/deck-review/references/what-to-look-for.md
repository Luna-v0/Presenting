# Reading a deck back

The mechanical pass has already run. This is what is left, and all of it is
judgement.

## Watch it, do not read it

Open the HTML export and click through at talk pace. Reading the screenplay
tells you what the deck says; watching it tells you whether it works. They
disagree more often than they agree.

## Per beat

- Does the frame match its `Claim`? The claim is never on screen, so this is the
  one check nothing else can do.
- Is there a beat you cannot say the point of in one sentence?
- Is the visual carrying the beat, or decorating it?
- Did a reveal land in the wrong order — something visible before the sentence
  that earns it?

## Per section

- Does the bridge still hold once you have watched it, not just read it?
- Does the section end somewhere, or just stop?
- Would cutting the section change what the audience leaves with? If not, that
  is a demotion candidate, not a defect.

## Across the deck

- Where does it drag? Note the beat id, not the feeling.
- Where does it jump? Usually a missing bridge that was written anyway.
- Is the arc the brief's `Narrative`, or did it drift into a survey?
- Time: does the render's slide count match what the slot can hold at talk pace?

## Turning reactions into verdicts

The user rarely says "demote". They say things like:

| What they say | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "this is really cool but it doesn't fit here" | `demote` |
| "this is fine but the whole middle is in the wrong order" | `narrative-wrong` on the beats, then reopen the brief |
| "I'd cut this" | ask first — `demote` unless they insist on deletion |
| "yeah, that works" | `keep` |

Record the sentence they used. Paraphrasing it loses the thing that makes the
beat retrievable next time.
